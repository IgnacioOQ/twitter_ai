Aesthetic concepts that may be found in tweets. These are not mutually exclusive and multiple concepts may feature in the same tweet.

**Value**   
There are different views on the value of art. Any tweet that mentions something being a good or bad artwork, or good or bad art, may be invoking the idea of the value of art. They may highlight whether creators are able to produce anything of value. They may also highlight flaws in a work, or strengths of a work. They may also use the language of liking or disliking, though this can overlap with tastes. NOTE: this does not mean monetary value \- the question of the value of art is not about whether something will sell.

**Intentionalism**   
Intentionalism about art is the view that the artist or author is the person who determines what a work of art means. When we discuss what a work means, under this view, we are discussing what the author intended it to mean. The author or artist is often considered the ultimate authority on what a work means. There are also ‘intentionalist’ views about the definition of art. This is the idea that something counts as art because the artist intended it to be art. Tweets that are evoking intentionalism might refer to what a work of art means, what is intended by the artist, or what the artist meant by the work. If articulating this in a critical way, a tweet may highlight the lack of meaning or intention due to the lack of an artist, the idea that something isn’t a work of art because there is no meaning or intention behind it, or it may highlight the shallow nature of the piece.   
e.g.: 

* can we please stop calling it "ai art" and start calling it "ai generated images" instead? art requires meaning and intention, ai has none of that   
  (high confidence \- evokes meaning and intention) 


**Anti-intentionalism**   
Anti-intentionalism about art is the view that the artist or author is NOT the person who determines what a work of art means, rather the meaning is set by the language or imagery used. This can be interpreted by critics and audiences. All interpretations may be legitimate unless they cannot be supported at all by the work. The author or artist is not the ultimate authority on what a work means. When we discuss what a work means, under this view, we are discussing what the author intended it to mean.  Tweets that are evoking anti-intentionalism might refer to what a words or image in a work of art means outside of the artist. They might mention that the artist’s intentions don’t matter. There might be explicit mention of the so-called ‘Death of the author or the work of Roland Barthes.

**Cognitivism**    
Cognitivism is the view that art is valuable because it gives us knowledge or understanding. Tweets that are showing a cognitivist point of view on art may highlight learning from art, art providing us with understanding, teaching us something, or communicating something we might not otherwise have had access to. It might invoke the importance of art or artists making us think, or being about ideas. In the negative, a tweet might highlight the shallowness of a work, the lack of understanding or the meaninglessness of a work.    
e.g. 

* gm ☕ i've been playing a lot with ai image generation lately. it's innovation and use cases are an absolute game-changer. i'm not a fantastic artist, but i like to think i'm a creative person, so for me it's an avenue to express those ideas and a journey of discovery.☄️ 

(high: mentions expressing ideas)   
   
**Expressivism**   
Expressivism is the view that art is value because it expresses emotion, or makes us feel emotions when we engage with it. It is sometimes formulated as a view about what the artist is feeling when they make art, or it can be more about what kind of emotion the work captures, or it can be about the emotion an artwork evokes in the audience. There is also view that expression and emotion are essential for art, and that this is a definitional component of art (i.e. what makes art art).  Tweets that show an expressivist view of art may highlight what the work makes them feel or what they think the artist was feeling whilst making it. They may highlight specific emotions such as sadness or anger as something recognisable within the work (NOTE: it is not enough for them to name an emotion they are feeling and then discuss art \- the feeling needs to be caused by the work). They may say that emotion or feeling is needed for something to be an artwork. In the negative, they may note the lack of emotion in a work as a negative, or highlight how the creator cannot feel or have emotions.   
e.g.: 

* ✨good morning friends\! i am happy to show you my new collection “creatures”, which is presented at the fnd✨ ✨ manifold contract, reserve 0,05 eth✨ 🖤 creatures \- this ai collection is more about feeling than about a fairy tale🖤   
  (medium: mentions a feeling)   
   

**Hedonism**   
Hedonism in art is about the pleasure we take from art. At a theoretical level, it is the idea that experiencing pleasure is the main value we take from art. Tweets which are referencing an idea of aesthetic hedonism may mention enjoyment from looking at art, the pleasure art gives us, art making us feel good etc. In the negative, a tweet might highlight how a work of art is unpleasant, ugly, etc, and therefore is bad.

**Originality**   
Originality in art refers to something which is non-trivially new in a work of art. It relates to ideas of creativity (in the positive) and ideas about copying (in the negative). Tweets that are referencing the importance of originality may mention creativity, novelty, newness, difference to prior works, or in the negative may reference theft, copying, tracing, plagiarising, replicating.

**Achievement and effort**   
Achievement may come in to discussions of art in a few ways. An achievement view of the definition of art may insist that art is always a kind of achievement. An achievement view of the value of art is that an artwork is valuable insofar as it is an artistic achievement. Skills or effort may also be highlighted as something valuable or essential to art, though this is a slightly different issue. A tweet invoking an achievement view of art might highlight how the work was an artistic achievement, how the artist is a master, or how there is a high level of skill involved. In the negative, this might be highlighting a lack of skill, a lack of artistic achievement, or a lack of hard work in learning to produce art.
